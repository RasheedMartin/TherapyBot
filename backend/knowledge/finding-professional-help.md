# Finding and starting professional mental health care

## When to consider a professional
It is worth reaching out when difficulties last for weeks, interfere with work, school, relationships, or sleep, or when self-help hasn't been enough. You don't need to be in crisis to deserve support.

## Types of professionals
- **Psychologists** (PhD/PsyD) provide therapy and psychological assessment.
- **Licensed counselors and therapists** (LPC, LMHC, LMFT) and **clinical social workers** (LCSW) provide talk therapy.
- **Psychiatrists** (MD/DO) and **psychiatric nurse practitioners** diagnose and can prescribe medication.
- A **primary care doctor** is often a good first stop and can screen, prescribe, and refer.

## How to find someone
- Check your insurance provider's directory, or call the number on your card for in-network referrals.
- Therapist directories let you filter by issue, approach (for example CBT or DBT), insurance, and cost.
- Lower-cost options include community mental health centers, university training clinics, and sliding-scale practices.
- In the US, the SAMHSA National Helpline (1-800-662-4357) offers free, confidential treatment referrals 24/7.
- Many workplaces and schools offer free short-term counseling through an employee assistance program or counseling center.

## What to ask a potential therapist
What approaches do you use for my concern? How long does treatment usually take? What are your fees and cancellation policy? Do you offer telehealth? It is fine to try a different therapist if the fit doesn't feel right; the relationship matters.

## Making the most of therapy
Write down what you want to work on, note patterns between sessions, practice skills as homework, and give honest feedback about what is or isn't helping.

## Crisis resources
For immediate danger call 911. For suicidal thoughts or emotional crisis, call or text 988 (US). Outside the US, findahelpline.com lists local services.
