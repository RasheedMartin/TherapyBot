# Sleep and a racing mind at night

## Why sleep and mood are linked
Poor sleep makes stress, anxiety, and low mood harder to manage, and worry makes sleep harder. Improving sleep habits is often one of the most effective first steps.

## Core habits
- Keep the same wake-up time every day, including weekends.
- Use the bed mainly for sleep, not for scrolling, work, or worrying.
- If you're awake for what feels like more than 20 minutes, get up and do something quiet in dim light, then return when sleepy.
- Limit caffeine after early afternoon and reduce alcohol, which disrupts sleep later in the night.
- Build a wind-down routine of 30 to 60 minutes with dimmer light and fewer screens.

## When thoughts race at night
Earlier in the evening, write down what's on your mind and one next step for each item, so the brain has less to "hold." In bed, try slow breathing, a body scan, or a mentally absorbing but boring task such as naming an animal for each letter of the alphabet.

## When to get help
Ongoing insomnia (several nights a week for three months or more), loud snoring with gasping, or severe daytime sleepiness are worth raising with a doctor. Cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is a well-established treatment.
