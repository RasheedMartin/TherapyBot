# Calming the body when anxiety spikes

## Why body-based skills help
Anxiety is partly a physical alarm response: faster heart rate, shallow breathing, tense muscles. Changing the body's state, especially breathing, can send a "safe" signal back to the brain. These skills work best when practiced regularly while calm, so they are familiar when stress hits.

## Slow, paced breathing
Breathe in through the nose for about 4 seconds and out slowly for about 6 seconds, for two to five minutes. A longer exhale than inhale tends to have the most calming effect. Box breathing (in 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4) is a popular alternative. If focusing on the breath makes someone more anxious, an outward-focused skill like grounding may suit them better.

## 5-4-3-2-1 grounding
Notice five things you can see, four you can feel, three you can hear, two you can smell, and one you can taste. This pulls attention out of racing thoughts and into the present moment. It is useful during panic, overwhelm, or when someone feels detached or "not really there."

## Progressive muscle relaxation
Working from the feet upward, tense one muscle group for about five seconds, then release it and notice the difference for about ten seconds. It helps people recognize where they hold tension and is often used before sleep.

## Before a stressful event
For interviews, exams, or difficult conversations: practice paced breathing beforehand, arrive early enough not to rush, and prepare a phrase to buy time ("Let me think about that for a moment"). Some nervousness is normal and can even sharpen performance; the goal is to keep it manageable, not to eliminate it.
